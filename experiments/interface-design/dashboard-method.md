# Large-screen decisions · make the important change visible

Use for a live or illustrative screen, not a generic website landing page or
a statistical claim reconstructed from a screenshot. Use the declared display
size and viewing distance. Deliver runnable HTML/CSS/JS and a preview; a native
draw.io file is not a default deliverable for this track.

## Choose the operational question

Start with what the audience must notice and what they do next: an incident,
regional imbalance, capacity constraint, comparison or trend. Pick one primary
working view. A map belongs in the centre only when location helps explain that
question; it is not an obligatory decoration for every industry.

Give the important view enough room, then place the few summaries and supporting
views needed to interpret it. Do not fill the border with tiny charts simply
because the canvas is wide. A symmetrical command-centre frame is one possible
composition, not a definition of a dashboard.

## Encode information consistently

- Let position, scale and ordering express comparison. Comparable views share
  appropriate axes and category identities. Mixed units need explicit scales.
- Separate category, status and measurement colours. A bright accent can mark
  an actual alert; using it on every border erases priority. Dark surfaces may
  suit the room, but blue glow is not evidence of technical sophistication.
- Use direct labels, units, date range, freshness and definitions where they
  affect interpretation. Keep secondary detail readable at the planned viewing
  size. Higher-resolution export does not repair tiny original type.
- Make chart choice follow the relationship. A ring is not a general substitute
  for comparison; 3D perspective must not distort quantities. A giant total
  without a meaningful period or denominator does not establish the story.
- Keep supporting frames, grid lines and textures subordinate. Reserve depth or
  an animated route for a real spatial/process relationship. Repeated luminous
  corners and moving particles are not a fallback for missing hierarchy.

## Be honest about data and behaviour

Use supplied data or a clearly identified local demonstration dataset. Never
invent a production value, geographic distribution, alarm, forecast or realtime
connection to complete the screen. Keep an empty/disconnected state distinct
from a measured zero. Preserve timestamps and units when provided.

Test filtering, selection, drill-down, reset and unavailable-data states only
when those actions are in scope. A static preview cannot verify interaction,
data correctness, large-screen viewing comfort or production deployment.

When studying a community template, note whether it is a useful composition,
an industry convention or a counterexample. A widely used template may still
have weak contrast, tiny labels, decoration competing with data or leftover
editor handles. Preserve that distinction before adapting it.

Exclude isolated 3D assets, scene baseplates and tutorial covers from screen
composition counts. A real screen preview can support layout observations;
it still does not verify its displayed numbers or live operational behaviour.

Reserve space for presentation modes. A presenter, subtitles, open help panel
or selected-object detail can cover the same central map the screen is meant to
explain. Define their occupied area and an unobstructed working view; hiding the
controls in a screenshot does not resolve that conflict.

A process topology may be a better main view than an administrative map when
the question concerns flows between equipment. For operating totals and work
orders, a chart-and-table composition can be sufficient. Choose the view from
the relationship, not from the expectation that every large screen needs a map.

Every encoding needs a reading key. Multicolour geographic grids, point height,
flow lines and heat fields should state what their colour, position or size
means, including units or a legend when needed. Do not infer that meaning from
a decorative preview. Likewise, a pale theme still needs text contrast; changing
a dark screen to pastel does not by itself improve its information hierarchy.

Study source: [EasyV public marketplace](https://easyv.cloud/market). Its previews
are reference material, not reusable production facts or a guarantee of quality.
Further per-case visual records and user review must test these decisions.
