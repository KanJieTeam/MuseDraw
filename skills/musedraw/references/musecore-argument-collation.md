# Supplied outlines and textual witnesses

`argument-outline` lays out one or two explicitly declared parallel or progressive
outlines. Each has an opening, two to five paragraph objects, optional supplied
evidence and an optional closing. Every branch, join and consecutive advance must
be supplied and agree with that declaration. It does not classify an article,
prove an argument or invent evidence. A parallel-section permutation count is a
property of the abstract declared graph, not a claim that all reordered prose
would read equally well.

`text-collation` accepts one short supplied example per selected teaching method:
same-work different-edition comparison, an internal parallel, another named work,
or reasoned conjecture. Work, edition, location and passage identities are
explicit. Unicode code-point LCS highlights textual differences; it does not
authenticate witnesses, infer copying history or recover an original reading.
Editorial adoption stays an editorial choice. Reasoned emendation is always
marked as a hypothesis, never a confirmed original. The four-method display order
is not an obligatory workflow or automatic confidence ranking.

Use `core example argument-outline` or `core example text-collation`, inspect the
JSON fields, then render through `core render`. The renderer coordinates paper,
ink, paragraph objects, differences and labels. Unsupported graph structures,
long passages or missing witness identities need another route. Geometry checks
do not judge literary reasoning, scholarship or aesthetic quality.
