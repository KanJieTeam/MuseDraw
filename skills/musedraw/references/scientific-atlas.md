# Scientific visual families

Read the one row matching the figure's job, then the matching method. These
families organise visual decisions; they are not a promise that a title alone
supplies scientific geometry or data. A Nature figure can contain several
families, with one dominant question and subordinate evidence.

| Visual job | Composition and craft | MuseDraw entry / required inputs |
|---|---|---|
| One object changes state | Reuse its geometry and identity; align the unchanged boundaries. Make deformation, contact, position or presence carry the difference. Keep labels beside the changed region. | `object-views`, `layers`, `laminate`; explicit states and fixed anchors. See [object composition](object-composition.md). |
| A mechanism connects actors | Let objects occupy the real interaction space; distinguish process, feedback, physical contact and explanatory leaders. A branch label belongs on that actual branch. | `graph`, ordinary native nodes/edges and formulas; supplied actors, signs and topology. A generic graph does not validate a mechanism. |
| A computational method transforms representations | Make the representation visible: sequence, feature array, graph or latent geometry. Show shared consumers once and put local detail near the relevant operation. | `tensor`, `sequence`, `graph`, `network`, `manifold`, `formula`; explicit dimensions/topology where they matter. Use the closest original method, not its unrelated branches. |
| An apparatus explains a measurement | Give the experiment a spatial arrangement, then attach source, sample, sensing region and detector to their actual locations. Distinguish the optical/control path from the measured response. | Native paths/ports/layers and annotation; supplied apparatus geometry and component identities. True dimensions, calibration and optical laws need evidence. |
| A process tracks ownership or cohort flow | Preserve start, branch, exclusion, crossover and rejoin. Peers share baselines; short side branches keep their association. A compact flow can be the right design. | Semantic `compose`, swimlane or ordinary connected edges. Counts, cohort definitions and exclusions come from the brief; drawing a flow does not prove conservation. |
| A map explains transport or a spatial field | Keep geography as the base, place routes at actual coordinates, and separate source identity from the quantity encoded by width or colour. | Supplied vector geography/coordinates, native paths and labels. Terrain rasters and real fields need their original assets/data; do not invent a geographic silhouette. |
| Sites or sequence features differ by condition | Reuse the same substrate, direction and phase. Change only specified intervals or marks. Let parent conditions span their actual subcases and share one identity legend. | `sequence`, `duplex`; explicit sites/strands/marks. See [scientific comparisons](scientific-comparisons.md). No implied complementary mark, binding interface or sequence. |
| A molecular structure explains a transformation | Preserve atom identity, bond order, stereochemistry and unchanged fragments. Put the changed bond or contact where the argument happens. | Supplied molecular topology/coordinates; graph/paths can depict it, but current generic graph motifs do not implement a chemistry validator or molecular renderer. |
| Several quantitative panels answer one question | Assign each panel an evidence role. Comparable panels keep group identities and compatible scales; uncertainty and sample definitions stay attached to their data. | Real data and an appropriate plotting backend; MuseDraw can organise labels and schematic context. Never reconstruct statistics from a preview's dot positions. |
| Microscopy or imaging is the evidence | Preserve the actual image, channel identity, scale bar and overview-to-detail region. Keep annotation subordinate to the evidence. | Actual authorised source images and registration information. Fully native schematic export does not reproduce empirical pixels; disclose the output boundary. |
| A dense table or mapping is the argument | Keep row/column identities and a precise legend. Use space to expose the mapping; colour alone cannot replace the entries. | Native table/grid or explicit cells with the supplied mapping. A shared colour is not proof that two entries are equivalent. |
| Different evidence types work together | Put the mechanism/overview in charge, then make images, plots and details answer its specific questions. Use unequal panel spans when their roles need different room. | Compose the relevant routes. Keep source data/assets and schematic annotations distinct; a homogeneous grid is not automatically a coherent evidence chain. |

## Cross-family decisions

When overview and detail coexist, select the actual relation in the
[detail method](scientific-details.md): same geometry, induced subgraph,
representation mapping and sample association have different contracts.

- **Visual weight follows the question.** A large object, decisive interaction
  or primary path can lead; headings, legends and guides remain subordinate.
  Do not assign the largest area to the longest paragraph by default.
- **Use colour roles, not a universal palette.** A protein identity, a treatment
  group, a continuous measurement and a process arrow are different encodings.
  Keep one mapping across panels. Red may be a methylation mark, a beam or an
  error depending on the supplied figure; never transfer its meaning blindly.
- **Use geometry to reduce prose.** A contact face, shared route, repeated
  baseline, aligned interval or changed site can show a relationship directly.
  Retain necessary scientific labels; remove text that merely narrates the
  construction of the drawing.
- **Use line weight consistently.** Material boundaries, main relationships,
  guides and group separators have different jobs. Keep their weights distinct
  and test legibility at the intended output size. Uniformly thick arrows can
  overpower the object; uniformly faint lines can erase the mechanism.
- **Scale for the actual destination.** Inspect the planned screen/print size,
  including the smallest meaningful mark. Making a 2× PNG does not fix a label
  that was already too small in the scene.
- **An exact-looking picture is not an exact model.** A reference's smooth
  curve, surface or shaded object can be measured evidence or a conceptual
  cartoon. Preserve that distinction before adapting its visual language.

## Examples behind the methods

These are source studies, not bundled templates or reusable experiment facts:

- [Fixed-substrate condition comparison](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41477-026-02396-4/figures/6):
  parent conditions, repeated DNA and explicit mark/actor differences.
- [Apparatus and measurement coordinates](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43246-020-0007-4/figures/1):
  a sample/measurement arrangement needs its spatial geometry, not just steps.
- [Sequence and spatial correspondence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-026-03171-6/figures/1):
  feature tracks need shared location references.
- [Contact and transfer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-026-01670-9/figures/1):
  distinguish the actual tools, moving material and retained pattern.
- [Optical mechanism and experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41566-026-02024-9/figures/1):
  energy transitions and spatial beam paths use related identities but different geometry.

Some source figures require real microscopy, quantitative data or spatial assets
outside a fully native schematic. Learn their hierarchy and correspondence
methods without replacing their evidence with a fabricated drawing.
