# Literary motif posters and explicit syntax sheets

`beiying-motif-poster` retains a two-ink platform/back-view motif with an attributed sequence of textual occurrences. `classical-syntax-sheet` retains a paper-and-red-annotation reading sheet. They share measured typography, not a generic literary-analysis model.

- Defaults are in `assets/musecore/beiying-motif-poster.json` and `classical-syntax-sheet.json`.
- The Beiying poster selects two to four of four verified occurrences in source order, retaining their original occurrence numbers. Reading labels are explicitly supplied interpretations.
- Coat width, hand span, lean and raised-foot controls reconstruct a bounded climbing motif. It is not a universal pose generator, railway reconstruction, anatomical measurement or psychology inference.
- The syntax sheet accepts one to six entries, using a finite classroom catalogue or explicitly supplied analysis. Code-point spans identify cue marks; token permutation and separately supplied additions are checked against their input.
- Original wording, a title fragment, reordered reading and bracketed teaching additions have different identities. Supplied additions must never appear as authenticated original wording.
- A particle or visual cue alone does not establish a universal grammar rule. The programme does not parse, translate or authenticate arbitrary classical Chinese.
- Reject unsupported source selections, inconsistent token/span operations and capacity excess. Grow measured space or exit; do not cut quotations, invent a reading or shrink text to preserve old coordinates.

Use the ordinary core render command. Keep input, source references and SVG/PNG together. Inspect both whole-page composition and delivery-size legibility; arithmetic and geometry checks are separate from aesthetic judgement.
